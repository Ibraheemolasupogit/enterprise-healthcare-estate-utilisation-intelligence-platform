# Business Problem

Healthcare estate decisions require more than room-booking evidence. A booked room may have been unused, used for a
different purpose, unsuitable for a service, dependent on a constrained workforce group, or essential as contingency
capacity. Treating bookings as utilisation can therefore create false confidence.

Evidence is commonly fragmented across estates registers, booking systems, clinical activity records, workforce plans,
finance ledgers, accessibility evidence and local operational knowledge. The future platform is intended to link those
synthetic evidence streams so that decision-makers can understand current use, future demand, feasible options, cost,
risk and uncertainty.

Stakeholders may legitimately value different outcomes. Clinical teams may prioritise service continuity and specialist
rooms. Finance may need transparent cost assumptions. Estates may focus on condition, capacity and running costs.
Patients and accessibility representatives may emphasise travel burden and equitable access. Executives need a decision
view that shows trade-offs rather than a single simplistic utilisation percentage.

The future decision will be whether proposed estate changes are operationally feasible, evidence-supported and safe to
take through governance. Patient access, service continuity, workforce feasibility and operational resilience must limit
any option before nominal savings are considered.
